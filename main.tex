\title{Does Your AI Coding Plan Still Buy What It Used~To? \\
A Purchasing-Power Index for Agentic Coding Subscriptions}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Lucas Andrade Zanetti}
\IEEEauthorblockA{\textit{Faculdade de Ci\^{e}ncias e Tecnologias em Engenharia (FCTE)} \\
\textit{Universidade de Bras\'{i}lia} \\
Bras\'{i}lia, Brazil \\
landradezanetti@gmail.com}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Developers report that their fixed-price AI coding subscription ``does less
than it used to,'' while vendors report ever-improving benchmark scores. We
show that both can be true: benchmarks are \emph{capability-denominated} (they
price a model), whereas a subscription is \emph{quota-denominated} (it prices
an entitlement consumed by an agent harness whose appetite drifts). We define
the Real Task Purchasing power $\RTP=Qs/c$ and an exact additive decomposition
of its log change into entitlement, capability, consumption and price, of
which only price is public. Three structural results follow: concave
test-time scaling forces the quota cost per solved task upward; a falsifiable
Jevons condition governs energy per solved task; and quota compression is the
profit-maximising response to fat-tailed agentic usage. We specify a
reproducible measurement protocol (a normalised quota unit, an entitlement
probe, a frozen harness, a drift canary, pre-registered hypotheses) and show
that resolving a $10\%$ value change needs roughly \nReqTen{} tasks, so an
individual developer's experience is underpowered by about two orders of
magnitude. The design-analysis numbers come from a declared simulation. A
small measured pilot of a reference implementation, run on the author's own
accounts for three clients, shows that the instrument works end to end and how
differently clients expose consumption; it does not measure plan value.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
AI coding assistants, agentic software engineering, cost-aware evaluation,
benchmarking, index numbers, energy efficiency, software economics.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:scope}

A recurring complaint follows every generation of AI coding tools: \emph{the
plan I pay for feels worse than it did six months ago}. The usual rebuttal is
also true: coding benchmark scores keep
rising~\cite{jimenez2024swebench,jain2025livecodebench}. Both sides argue past
each other because they quote prices in different currencies. A benchmark
score answers a \emph{capability-denominated} question: with an unstated
budget, what fraction of tasks does this model solve? A subscriber asks a
\emph{quota-denominated} one: given what my monthly fee buys, how much
engineering work can I ship before I am throttled? The two are linked by three
factors that drift, mostly silently: the \textbf{entitlement} $Q$ the plan
grants, in units the vendor defines and may redefine; the \textbf{capability}
$s$ of whichever model the plan routes to on the day, which is not
contractually fixed and measurably moves~\cite{chen2024drift}; and the
\textbf{consumption} $c$ an agent harness burns per attempt, which has grown
by orders of magnitude with chain-of-thought, tool loops and test-repair
cycles~\cite{wei2022cot,snell2024scaling,yang2024sweagent}. Only the price $P$
is contractual and public, so a plan can be devalued to half its usefulness
without a word on the pricing page changing, as a currency can be debased
without altering the number printed on the note.

We therefore treat a subscription as a good whose real price should be tracked
with consumer-price machinery~\cite{fisher1922index,ilo2004cpi}: a fixed task
basket, an index over time, a substitution-bias analysis and a quality
adjustment. The novel object is the denominator: instead of pricing the basket
in dollars, we price it in the plan's own entitlement.

\textbf{Related work.} Code benchmarks moved from function
synthesis~\cite{chen2021humaneval,austin2021mbpp} to repository-level issue
resolution~\cite{jimenez2024swebench,openai2024swebenchverified} and rolling,
contamination-resistant sets~\cite{jain2025livecodebench}; their scores come
from a scaffold~\cite{yang2024sweagent,wang2025openhands} and are reported per
model, usually without budgets. Cost-aware evaluation~\cite{kapoor2024agents,%
chen2023frugalgpt,liang2023helm} denominates cost in API dollars, the wrong
unit for flat-rate subscribers whose binding constraint is an unobserved
entitlement; to our knowledge no prior work measures the subscription rather
than the model. Per-token inference energy has fallen~\cite{kwon2023vllm,%
leviathan2023speculative,samsi2023watts,luccioni2024powerhungry} while
aggregate demand rises~\cite{masanet2020recalibrating,iea2025energyai}, the
Jevons structure~\cite{jevons1865coal}. Field evidence on productivity is
mixed~\cite{peng2023copilot,brynjolfsson2025genai,metr2025slowdown}. We borrow
Laspeyres, Paasche and Fisher indices~\cite{fisher1922index,ilo2004cpi},
substitution bias~\cite{boskin1996toward} and hedonic
adjustment~\cite{triplett2006hedonic}, and read the market through Akerlof's
lemons~\cite{akerlof1970lemons}.

\textbf{Contributions.} (i) A value model built on a \emph{Normalised Quota
Unit} and an exact four-channel decomposition (Section~\ref{sec:model}).
(ii) Three structural results on why value can fall while capability rises
(Section~\ref{sec:why}). (iii) \textsc{Planmeter}, a measurement protocol
(Section~\ref{sec:protocol}). (iv) A closed-form design analysis of the
evidence a claim of devaluation needs (Section~\ref{sec:sim}). (v) A reference
implementation with adapters for three coding-assistant clients and a small
real pilot (Section~\ref{sec:harness}). (vi) A one-page disclosure artifact,
the \emph{Plan Value Card} (Section~\ref{sec:card}).

\textbf{Scope.} This is an instrument paper. Every number in
Section~\ref{sec:sim} is the output of a simulation with declared parameters
(Table~\ref{tab:scenario}) and is not a measurement of any commercial plan.
Section~\ref{sec:harness} separately reports a small \emph{measured} pilot,
labelled as such, far too underpowered to estimate plan value
(Appendix~\ref{app:power}); no vendor price is reproduced, and we do not assert
that any devaluation has occurred.

\section{A Value Model for Coding Subscriptions}
\label{sec:model}

\begin{definition}[Task basket]
A basket $\mathcal{B}=\{(b_i,w_i)\}$, $\sum_i w_i = 1$, where each $b_i$ is a
software-engineering task pinned to a repository commit with a
machine-checkable oracle (tests, a differential oracle, or a build-and-lint
gate). A \emph{harness} $h$ is the full client-side configuration: agent loop,
tools, context and retry policy, step limit, temperature and model selector.
\end{definition}

A run of $b_i$ under plan $p$, harness $h$ and epoch $t$ (a calendar month)
yields oracle success $x_i\in\{0,1\}$, quota consumed $u_i>0$, tokens
$\tau_i$ (prefill, decode, cached-read, cached-write) and a review-quality
score $\kappa_i\in[0,1]$. Vendors meter in incomparable units and redefine
them, so we define the unit endogenously.

\begin{definition}[\NQU]
Fix a calibration task $b_0$ and reference harness $h_0$, frozen for the life
of the study. One \NQU{} is the quota consumed by one execution of
$(b_0,h_0)$. The entitlement $Q(p,t)$ is the number of such executions
completable in one billing epoch before the plan throttles.
\end{definition}

This makes $Q$ an \emph{observable} even when the vendor discloses nothing
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:probe}), and makes meter redefinition detectable, since
\NQU{} is anchored to $b_0$'s token counts. With $\bar x_i,\bar u_i$ the means
over $k$ repetitions, define basket solve rate and consumption per attempt
$s(t)\eqdef\sum_i w_i\bar x_i$, $c(t)\eqdef\sum_i w_i\bar u_i$, the quota cost
per solve and the work a plan buys per epoch,
\begin{equation}
\CPS(t) \eqdef \frac{c(t)}{s(t)}, \qquad
\RTP(p,t) \eqdef \frac{Q(p,t)\, s(t)}{c(t)},
\label{eq:rtp}
\end{equation}
and value per dollar
\begin{equation}
V(p,t) \eqdef \frac{\RTP(p,t)}{P(p,t)} = \frac{Q(p,t)\,s(t)}{c(t)\,P(p,t)} .
\label{eq:value}
\end{equation}
The index is $\CPPI(p,t;t_0)\eqdef V(p,t)/V(p,t_0)$ with continuously
compounded \emph{debasement rate} $\delta\eqdef-\ln\CPPI/(t-t_0)$: a plan with
$\delta=0.30\,\text{yr}^{-1}$ loses a quarter of its real value per year at an
unchanged sticker price.

\begin{proposition}[Exact additive decomposition]
\label{prop:decomp}
For any two epochs,
\begin{equation}
\underbrace{\Delta \ln V}_{\text{value}} =
\underbrace{\Delta \ln Q}_{\text{entitlement}} +
\underbrace{\Delta \ln s}_{\text{capability}} -
\underbrace{\Delta \ln c}_{\text{consumption}} -
\underbrace{\Delta \ln P}_{\text{price}} .
\label{eq:decomp}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{IEEEproof}
Immediate from~\eqref{eq:value} and the additivity of $\ln$; it is exact, not
a first-order approximation.
\end{IEEEproof}

Vendors quote the second term, users feel the sum, and the first and third,
which have moved most, are unobservable outside the vendor.

\textbf{Quality adjustment.} A green test suite lower-bounds value. Following
hedonic practice~\cite{triplett2006hedonic} we also report a quality-weighted
rate $\tilde s\eqdef\sum_i w_i\,\overline{x_i\kappa_i}$, with $\kappa$ from a
frozen, human-calibrated rubric judge~\cite{zheng2023judge} (a drifting judge
contaminates the capability channel). Reporting $s$ and $\tilde s$ separates
``it passes'' from ``I would merge it''~\cite{metr2025slowdown}.

\textbf{Substitution bias and infeasibility.}

Users re-optimise when quota binds, so a fixed-harness index overstates the
loss as a fixed-basket CPI overstates inflation~\cite{boskin1996toward}. With
$h_{t_0}$ the base harness and $h_t$ the re-optimised one,
$L(t)=V(h_{t_0},t)/V(h_{t_0},t_0)$ and $Pa(t)=V(h_t,t)/V(h_t,t_0)$, and the
Fisher index $F=\sqrt{L\,Pa}$~\cite{fisher1922index} is the headline.

\begin{proposition}[Infeasibility under deprecation]
\label{prop:deprecation}
If the model or endpoint available at $t_0$ is withdrawn before $t$, the
denominator $V(h_t,t_0)$ is unmeasurable, so $Pa(t)$ and $F(t)$ do not exist;
only the Laspeyres bound survives, and only while the base harness is runnable.
\end{proposition}

A vendor that retires endpoints faster than the study window destroys the
comparability of its own product.

\section{Why Value Can Fall While Capability Rises}
\label{sec:why}

\textbf{Concavity.} Let $s(\tau)$ be the solve rate at per-attempt token
budget $\tau$ and $\eta_{s,\tau}\eqdef(\mathrm{d}s/\mathrm{d}\tau)(\tau/s)$ its
elasticity.

\begin{proposition}[Rising cost per solved task]
\label{prop:elasticity}
$\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\left(\dfrac{\tau}{s(\tau)}\right) > 0
\iff \eta_{s,\tau} < 1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{IEEEproof}
$\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\frac{\tau}{s}=\frac{s-\tau s'}{s^2}
=\frac{1}{s}(1-\eta_{s,\tau})$ and $s>0$.
\end{IEEEproof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:logscaling}
Under the log-linear test-time scaling reported for reasoning
models~\cite{snell2024scaling,deepseek2025r1}, $s\approx a+b\ln\tau$ gives
$\eta_{s,\tau}=b/s\to0$ as $s$ saturates, so cost per solved task grows
asymptotically linearly in the budget: each extra point of accuracy costs more
than the last.
\end{corollary}

Fig.~\ref{fig:elasticity} shows this on a saturating curve with shape
$\gamma=\gammaHill$: unit cost is minimal at $\tau^\star\approx\tauStar\times
10^3$ tokens and rises thereafter; at $600\times10^3$ tokens, ordinary for a
repository-level agent run, it is already $\cpsRatio\times$ the minimum.

\textbf{The progress trap.} At fixed price, Proposition~\ref{prop:decomp}
makes the trade-off two-dimensional: value is unchanged exactly on the locus
$\Delta\ln s=\Delta\ln c-\Delta\ln Q$. With an entitlement change of
$\Delta\ln Q=\scendlnQ$, every point with a capability gain below
$\Delta\ln c-\Delta\ln Q$ has capability strictly improving while value
strictly falls, and no party is lying; the declared scenario of
Table~\ref{tab:scenario} sits there.

\textbf{Energy (task-level Jevons).}
\label{sec:jevons}
With $e(t)$ the marginal energy per token and $\bar\tau(t)$ the mean tokens
per attempt, energy per solved task is $\EPS=\bar\tau e/s$.

\begin{proposition}[Task-level rebound]
\label{prop:jevons}
$\EPS$ increases between two epochs if and only if
\begin{equation}
\Delta\ln\bar\tau-\Delta\ln s>-\Delta\ln e .
\label{eq:jevons}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

Serving efficiency helps at the task level only if per-token efficiency
improves faster than agent verbosity net of capability. The condition is
falsifiable: $\bar\tau$ and $s$ are measured, and $e$ is bounded bottom-up,
$e=\mathrm{PUE}\cdot\overline{\mathrm{P}}_{\text{node}}
(\phi/\Lambda_{\text{prefill}}+(1-\phi)/\Lambda_{\text{decode}})$, with
$\Lambda$ the measured throughput per phase and $\phi$ the prefill share,
reported as an interval~\cite{samsi2023watts,luccioni2024powerhungry,%
henderson2020systematic}; it is an external bound, never a vendor disclosure. In the declared scenario a $\eGain\%$ drop in energy
per token is more than cancelled by $\tauGrowth\times$ more tokens per
attempt, leaving energy per solved task $\epsChange\%$ higher after twelve
months, with slack $\jevonsSlack$ in~\eqref{eq:jevons}.

\textbf{The vendor's side.} Devaluation need not be bad faith. Let usage $U$
be Pareto with tail index $\alpha$ and scale $1$, the shape once a minority of
users run agents overnight. The vendor sets quota $Q$ at marginal cost
$\kappa$ per \NQU; retention is $R(Q)=Q^\theta/(Q^\theta+Q_c^\theta)$. Expected
profit per subscriber is $\pi(Q)=R(Q)[P-\kappa\,m(Q)]$ with
$m(Q)=\mathbb{E}[\min(U,Q)]$ and $m'(Q)=Q^{-\alpha}$.

\begin{proposition}[Rational quota compression]
\label{prop:vendor}
Any interior optimum satisfies
\begin{equation}
\frac{R'(Q)}{R(Q)}=\frac{\kappa\,Q^{-\alpha}}{P-\kappa\,m(Q)},
\label{eq:foc}
\end{equation}
and one exists precisely when $\alpha<1+\theta$. Fattening the tail
(decreasing $\alpha$) raises the right-hand side pointwise and leaves the
left unchanged, so $Q^\star$ falls.
\end{proposition}
\begin{IEEEproof}
Differentiate $\ln\pi$ to get~\eqref{eq:foc}. As $Q\to\infty$,
$R'/R\sim\theta Q_c^\theta Q^{-(1+\theta)}$ while the right side decays as
$Q^{-\alpha}$; profit is eventually increasing iff $1+\theta<\alpha$. For the
comparative static, $\partial/\partial\alpha$ of the right side is negative for
$Q>1$, so decreasing $\alpha$ moves the crossing left.
\end{IEEEproof}

Solving~\eqref{eq:foc} numerically at $P=\priceRef$, $\kappa=\kappaMarg$,
$\theta=\thetaRet$, moving the tail index from $\alphaThin$ to $\alphaFat$ (the
qualitative effect of agentic use arriving on a chat-priced plan) drives the
optimal entitlement to $\times\QstarRel$ of its former level. Subscribers are
right that plans tighten and vendors are right that they must: the conflict is
structural, and flat rate for agentic workloads is a transitional regime
(Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\section{\textsc{Planmeter}: A Measurement Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

Each channel of the model must be estimable by an outside party with one
ordinary account.

\textbf{Basket.} We stratify by the shape of work, since consumption scales
with agent depth rather than syntax: S1 local repair (weight $0.30$), S2
multi-file feature ($0.35$), S3 repository-wide migration ($0.20$) and S4
long-horizon issue with a declared step cap ($0.15$). Three contamination
controls are mandatory, following~\cite{jain2025livecodebench}: tasks come
from commits after every tested model's training cutoff and the basket rolls
forward with a frozen legacy arm; a private hold-out third is never published;
and task prose is paraphrased with perturbed identifiers.

\textbf{Freezing the harness.}
\label{sec:harnessfix}
The easiest way to ruin the measurement is to let the client improve during
the study. We pin the agent loop, tool schemas, prompts, context and retry
policy, step limit and decoding, and run two arms each epoch. The \emph{frozen
arm} ($h_{t_0}$) isolates vendor-side change and estimates $\Delta\ln s$ and
$\Delta\ln c$ cleanly; the \emph{as-delivered arm} ($h_t$) is the vendor's
current default client, and its difference from the frozen arm is the value
the \emph{tooling} added or destroyed. Reporting only the first attributes
harness progress to the model; only the second hides model regressions behind
scaffold gains.

\textbf{Instrumentation.} Every run appends one JSON record: task and stratum,
harness arm and commit, advertised and routed model, oracle result, steps and
wall time, tokens by class (prefill, decode, reasoning, cache read), quota
meter before and after, and an energy interval, with \texttt{null} wherever the
vendor does not expose a field. Token classes stay separate because cached
reads are far cheaper than fresh prefill, so a cache-hit improvement would
otherwise look like a capability gain.

\textbf{Measuring an undisclosed entitlement.} $Q$ is the quantity the vendor
does not publish and the one that has moved most; it can be estimated by
saturation search on a single owned account (Algorithm~\ref{alg:probe}).

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Entitlement saturation probe}
\label{alg:probe}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require calibration task $b_0$, harness $h_0$, plan $p$, epoch budget, duty cycle $\rho$
\State $j\gets0$
\While{not throttled \textbf{and} epoch not exhausted}
  \State run $(b_0,h_0)$; record meter reading and latency; $j\gets j+1$
  \If{response indicates rate limit or tier downgrade}
     \State record throttle type, reset horizon, residual capability; \textbf{break}
  \EndIf
  \State sleep to respect $\rho$ \Comment{never exceed documented limits}
\EndWhile
\State \Return $\hat Q\gets j$ (in \NQU{}) and throttle class $\in$ \{hard stop, queue, silent downgrade\}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The throttle \emph{class} is itself a finding: a plan that silently routes to a
smaller model on exhaustion has not reduced $Q$ but $s$, a different channel of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:decomp}. Plans use rolling multi-scale windows, so $\hat Q$ is
reported per window with the binding one identified, and the probe runs at low
duty cycle and randomised times (Section~\ref{sec:ethics}).

\textbf{Drift canary and inference.} A small canary set of stable difficulty
runs more often than the full basket. Watching a stream for a step change, we
use a one-sided CUSUM~\cite{page1954cusum} with a threshold calibrated to a
declared in-control false-alarm rate; latency and response-format fingerprints
are covariates, and an alarm is a signal, never an identification of which
model served the request. Per epoch we compute $\hat s,\hat{\tilde s},\hat c$
and $\hat V=\hat Q\hat s/(\hat cP)$, bootstrap $10^4$ resamples \emph{over
tasks}, not runs, for the CI on $\ln\hat V$~\cite{efron1994bootstrap}, form
$L$, $Pa$ and $F$ (omitting the last two if Prop.~\ref{prop:deprecation}
applies), and report the decomposition~\eqref{eq:decomp} with per-channel CIs
under Benjamini--Hochberg control at $q=0.10$~\cite{benjamini1995fdr}. The five
hypotheses of Table~\ref{tab:hyp}, the basket, weights, arms and analysis
script are registered before the first epoch; anything else is exploratory.

\section{Reference Implementation and a Real Pilot}
\label{sec:harness}

This subsection reports numbers that were actually measured, on the author's
own accounts, with an open reference implementation of \textsc{Planmeter}: the
run-record schema; a vendor adapter that shells out to the client's own CLI and
never decides correctness; an independent oracle that overlays a private
held-out test only after the client has finished; the probe of
Algorithm~\ref{alg:probe}; a CUSUM canary; and the bootstrap and
Benjamini--Hochberg analysis. The basket is a \pilotNTasks{}-task pilot over all four
strata (S2--S4 with a private held-out test), run at $k=\pilotReps$ repetitions
on both arms, logged verbatim to JSONL. Every number below flows from the
published aggregates through \texttt{scripts/figures.py}; the code, adapters
and exact commands are in the public repository
\url{https://github.com/Bappoz/coding-plan-value} (\texttt{harness/RUNBOOK.md}), and
raw run logs stay private per Section~\ref{sec:ethics}.

\textbf{Copilot} (\copilotVerifyOraclePass/\copilotVerifyCalls{} calibration
runs solved on \copilotVerifyDate; no basket). It exposes no token counts but
a native quota unit, metered ``premium requests,'' the opposite asymmetry from
Claude Code. Its frozen arm could not be instantiated: every explicit model
selection was rejected because the account disables manual selection, even
for the model auto-routing was observed serving. This is the freezing
assumption of Section~\ref{sec:harnessfix} failing in practice: a harness can
be frozen only if the vendor's client permits pinning.

\textbf{Codex} (\codexNRuns{} basket runs on \codexModel). It reports token
counts, accepts a pinned model, and exposes a numeric meter, the percentage of
a rolling \codexMeterWindowDays-day window. That meter is quantised to
\codexMeterRes\% and does not move within a run, so per-run consumption is the
reported token total; over the whole basket it rose from \codexMeterStart\% to
\codexMeterEnd\% (\codexTokMillions\,M tokens). A numeric meter is necessary for
measuring $c$ per attempt but not sufficient. The two arms are
indistinguishable by construction: while the vendor default equals the pinned
model the as-delivered arm \emph{is} the frozen arm, and the contrast can
become non-null only when the default changes, the event it exists to detect.
Across the three clients, consumption is therefore measured through three
instruments (USD proxy, request count, token total), which is why \NQU{} is
defined relative to a frozen calibration task rather than any native unit.

On Codex the probe also ran to its throttle, the first time it terminates
here. It ran sequentially ($\rho=1$) for \codexProbeHours{} hours, a deliberate,
owner-authorised departure from the 5\% duty cycle: at 5\% it would have taken
\codexProbeDutyDays{} days, longer than the window itself, so the low-load
protocol and saturating a weekly window are incompatible. After
\codexProbeCycles{} completed cycles, all solved on one model, the client
refused the next request with an explicit usage-limit message, a hard stop, with
no capability degradation on the approach. The meter had read 100\% for
\codexProbeAtFull{} further completed cycles before the stop, so a displayed
100\% is not exhaustion. The meter advanced one point per
\codexProbePerPoint{} cycles of \codexProbeTokK{}k tokens, putting this
account's weekly entitlement near \codexProbeQ{} calibration-task equivalents:
a first $\hat Q$ in \NQU, for one account, one tier and one week, and a lower
bound if the account was used concurrently. After the owner reset the quota
manually, the next run succeeded on the same model with the meter at
\codexPostResetMeter\%.

\textbf{Canary.} A scheduled cloud routine ran $b_0$ \canaryCycles{} times
between \canaryFirstDay{} and \canaryLastDay{} on a pinned model;
\canarySolved{} were solved and the CUSUM ($p_0=\canaryPzero$,
$\mathrm{ARL}_0=\canaryArl$) raised \canaryAlarms{} alarms. A trivially easy
canary on a pinned model gives a CUSUM nothing to detect: this shows the
pipeline runs unattended, not that capability is stable. It is a separate,
non-poolable channel; agents sometimes misreported timestamps or the output
format, so cycles are scored from platform fire time and the logged test
outcome.

The pilot is deliberately not a measurement of $V$: it lacks $P$ (no real
vendor price is stored) and, for two of three clients, $\hat Q$. It shows that
the instrument runs end to end against real accounts and that its statistics
behave as designed: with $\hat s=\pilotSHatFrozen\%$ at $n=\pilotNTasks$ it has no discriminating
power, and the bootstrap says so. The $n\gtrsim\nReqTen$ tasks needed for a
$10\%$ effect is the gap between this pilot and a real epoch.

\textbf{Decomposition.} Fig.~\ref{fig:waterfall} applies
Proposition~\ref{prop:decomp}. Capability contributes $\rdlns$ and is the only
positive term; consumption ($\rdlnc$) and entitlement ($\rdlnQ$) overwhelm it.
The net is $\Delta\ln V=\scenTotal$: the plan keeps $\scenRatio\%$ of its value
per dollar, a loss of $\scenLoss\%$ at unchanged price, a debasement rate
$\delta=\scenDelta\,\text{yr}^{-1}$ and a half-life of $\scenHalfLife$ months. The
bars are assumed; the point is that one scalar resolves into four attributable
causes and a plan can lose over half its worth while every published
capability metric improves.

\textbf{How much evidence does a claim of devaluation need?} Model per-task
success as Bernoulli($p$), $p=\pSolve$, and per-run consumption as lognormal
with log-scale $\sigma=\sigLog$. Appendix~\ref{app:power} derives by the delta
method, for a value ratio $r$ at power $1-\beta$ and level $\alpha$ in a paired
two-epoch design with $k$ repetitions,
\begin{equation}
n \;\ge\; \frac{2\left(\frac{1-p}{p} + e^{\sigma^2}-1\right)
              \left(z_{1-\alpha/2}+z_{1-\beta}\right)^2}
             {k\,\left(\ln r\right)^2} .
\label{eq:power}
\end{equation}
With $\left(\frac{1-p}{p}+e^{\sigma^2}-1\right)=\varUnit$, $k=\kReps$,
$\alpha=0.05$, $1-\beta=0.8$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:power} checks the closed form
against Monte Carlo):

Resolving a $10\%$ devaluation takes about \nReqTen{} distinct tasks, roughly
\runsTen{} agent runs across the two epochs. A developer completes a few
hundred non-trivial tasks a year, never repeats one under control, and keeps
changing their prompting habits, so their personal evidence is underpowered by
about two orders of magnitude. The developer who says the plan got worse cannot
prove it, the vendor who says benchmarks improved answers a different
question, and anecdotes of \emph{improvement} are equally uninformative.

\section{Threats to Validity and Ethics}
\label{sec:ethics}

\textbf{Validity.} A basket is not your repository: solve rate overstates
value where tests are weak and understates it where a correct patch fails the
oracle, and an LLM judge imports its own biases~\cite{zheng2023judge}. Serving
is nondeterministic, so repetitions are the unit of noise, and a server-side
A/B assignment lasting an epoch is indistinguishable from a global change on
one account, hence several independent accounts and regions. The frozen arm
ages, so both arms are reported. Eq.~\eqref{eq:power} ignores the design effect
of correlated repetitions and is a lower bound on $n$; multi-epoch monitoring
is sequential and must not be run as independent $t$-tests. The energy bound
is external with wide intervals and supports directional claims only. The
pilot of Section~\ref{sec:harness} is a feasibility demonstration, not
evidence about any plan: \pilotNTasks{} tasks, a solve-rate ceiling, one account per
vendor, one week for the one entitlement estimate. Each adapter's field
mapping was verified against real local runs only; a transcript relayed from a
separate agent session proved wrong in two fields.

\textbf{Ethics and terms of service.} The probe drives an account to its limit,
so it runs only on accounts the experimenter owns and pays for, respects
documented limits, never circumvents a throttle, CAPTCHA or authentication
control, uses low duty cycle and randomised timing, and does not probe beyond
what an ordinary client observes. We publish the \emph{method} and the
\emph{aggregate index}, never material that would help abuse a service; a
systematic discrepancy between advertised and measured entitlement calls for
responsible disclosure with a fixed embargo; environmental figures are external
estimates and must be labelled so.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{Flat rate is a transitional regime.} Proposition~\ref{prop:vendor}
says a fixed price cannot survive a usage tail set by unattended agents. Stable
forms are metered pricing, a hybrid with included allowance and transparent
overage, or explicit insurance with a deductible; each is more honest than a
flat plan with an undisclosed, shifting cap, because each makes $Q$
contractual. \textbf{The axis shifts to work per joule and per dollar.} By
Corollary~\ref{cor:logscaling} headline accuracy saturates and grows costlier,
while $\CPS$ and $\EPS$ do not: halving tokens per solve at constant accuracy
beats two benchmark points, and only the second is legible to buyers.
\textbf{Deprecation is index destruction.} Proposition~\ref{prop:deprecation}
calls for archived reference endpoints, frozen and available at cost, as
metrology keeps reference artefacts; without them every retrospective
comparison rests on scores that can never be re-run. \textbf{Transparency is
consumer protection.} Unit pricing exists because buyers cannot compare a
\SI{750}{\gram} package to a \SI{1}{\kilogram} one; here the package size is
unprinted and can change after purchase. The minimal ask is disclosure of the
entitlement in a stable unit and of the withdrawal notice period; absent it,
buyers discount every plan by the devaluation they cannot verify, and the
honest vendor cannot prove it~\cite{akerlof1970lemons}. Finally, $\RTP$
measures throughput of solved tasks, not engineering value~\cite{metr2025slowdown};
$\tilde s$ is a partial answer, and a full one needs field measurement of
merged, surviving code.

\section{Conclusion}

The question in the title has no answer today, and that is the finding. A
subscription is one public number and three private ones that move. We gave it
a value function, an exact four-channel decomposition, and three structural
results: unit cost of work must rise once test-time scaling turns concave;
energy per solved task can rise while energy per token falls; and compressing
entitlement is the profit-maximising response to agentic usage, so devaluation
needs no villain. We specified the instrument and showed that an individual
developer would need on the order of \nReqTen{} controlled tasks to prove a
$10\%$ effect, which is why the argument never resolves. What we did not do is
run it at the scale an index needs. The reference implementation was exercised
on three clients (Section~\ref{sec:harness}) and shows that each meters
consumption differently, but the pilot has $n=\pilotNTasks$ tasks, sits at a solve-rate
ceiling, reached a throttle on one of three clients, and measures neither $Q$
for the other two nor $P$ for any: it demonstrates feasibility and
underpowering, not devaluation. A full epoch is left as an open, reproducible
protocol, since an index is worth something only if maintained by someone other
than the seller.

\section{Sample size for the value ratio}
\label{app:power}

Let $\hat s$ be the mean of $nk$ Bernoulli($p$) draws and $\hat c$ the mean of
$nk$ lognormal draws with log-scale $\sigma$. By the delta method
$\operatorname{Var}(\ln\hat s)\approx\frac{1-p}{p\,nk}$ and
$\operatorname{Var}(\ln\hat c)\approx\frac{e^{\sigma^2}-1}{nk}$, so
$T=\ln\hat s-\ln\hat c$ has
$\operatorname{Var}(T)=\left(\frac{1-p}{p}+e^{\sigma^2}-1\right)/(nk)$ and the
two-epoch contrast $T_1-T_0$ twice that. Requiring
$|\ln r|\ge(z_{1-\alpha/2}+z_{1-\beta})\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(T_1-T_0)}$ and
solving for $n$ gives~\eqref{eq:power}. Correlated repetitions inflate the
variance by $1+(k-1)\rho_{\text{ICC}}$, so~\eqref{eq:power} is a lower bound;
estimate $\rho_{\text{ICC}}$ from a pilot epoch and scale accordingly.
